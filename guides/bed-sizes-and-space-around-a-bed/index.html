Bed Sizes and How Much Space You Need Around a Bed

A queen in New York, a king in London and a 160 in Berlin are three different beds. Here are the sizes, and the room you need around them.

Quick answer: Leave at least 60 cm (24 in) beside each side of the bed you use and at the foot, and 75 to 90 cm (30 to 35 in) if you can. In front of a wardrobe or a chest of drawers, leave room for the doors or drawers to open and for you to stand: around 90 cm (35 in).

European mattress sizes

Most of continental Europe names a bed by its width in centimetres, almost always 200 cm long.

These are mattress sizes. The frame adds to them: a few centimetres for a simple frame, 10 cm or more on each side for a padded or chunky one. Measure the frame you are buying, not the mattress.

How much space around the bed

  • The sides you get in from: 60 cm (24 in) at least, 75 to 90 cm (30 to 35 in) to move comfortably.
  • The foot: 60 cm (24 in) to walk past. More if a wardrobe or a dresser faces it.
  • Against a wall: pushing one side to the wall saves a walkway in a small room, but whoever sleeps there climbs over, and making the bed gets harder.
  • Nightstands: most are 40 to 60 cm (16 to 24 in) wide. Two of them add up fast next to a wide bed.
  • Wardrobes and drawers: leave space for doors and drawers to open fully, plus room to stand in front of them.
