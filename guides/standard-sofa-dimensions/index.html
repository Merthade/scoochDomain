Standard Sofa Dimensions, in cm and Inches

There is no official sofa size, but most sofas land in the same ranges. Here they are, with the numbers worth checking before you order one.

Quick answer: A typical 3-seater sofa is 190 to 230 cm (75 to 91 in) wide, 85 to 100 cm (33 to 39 in) deep and 75 to 90 cm (30 to 35 in) tall. A 2-seater is about 140 to 175 cm (55 to 69 in) wide. Use these to shortlist, then go by the listing: two sofas with the same number of seats can differ by 40 cm.

Sofa sizes by number of seats

Width is measured arm to arm, depth from the back to the front of the arm or seat, and height from the floor to the top of the back.

These are typical ranges, not rules. A deep lounge sofa can pass 110 cm, and a compact apartment sofa can sit under 85 cm.

The other numbers on the listing

  • Seat height: usually 40 to 48 cm (16 to 19 in) from the floor. Lower feels loungier and is harder to get up from.
  • Seat depth: usually 50 to 60 cm (20 to 24 in) from the front edge to the back cushion. Deeper suits sitting with your feet up; shorter suits shorter legs.
  • Arm height: usually 55 to 70 cm (22 to 28 in). It matters if a side table or a window sill sits next to it.
  • Sofa bed, open: most reach about 200 to 220 cm (79 to 87 in) from the back of the sofa to the foot of the mattress. Leave room to walk past the foot as well.
  • Diagonal depth: the number that decides whether it gets through your door. The sofa fit calculator shows how to measure it.

How much room to leave around a sofa

The sofa is only half the footprint. Common rules of thumb:

  • 35 to 45 cm (14 to 18 in) between the sofa and the coffee table.
  • 75 to 90 cm (30 to 35 in) for a walkway people use every day, and at least 60 cm (24 in) for a short squeeze.
  • A few centimetres between the back and the wall, more if a radiator is behind it.

There is more on this in how much space to leave between a sofa, coffee table and TV.
