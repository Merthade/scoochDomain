Where Keyplan 3D is stronger

Any shape

Walls go wherever you draw them, handy for an odd-shaped house. Most rooms are rectangles, though, and an L or a U is two or three of them joined, which is how Scooch builds them.

3D and decoration

Edit in 3D and paint or texture any surface. Good for trying a colour; it does not change whether the wardrobe door clears the bed.

Price

Its Standard version is $5.99 once, less than Scooch's $9.99. Object packs are extra, from $1.99 each, so two of them bring it to about Scooch's price.

Where Scooch is stronger

A warning before you lift anything

Scooch outlines in red a piece that overlaps another or sticks out of the room. Keyplan's listing does not describe a check like that, and we did not get to test one.

Exact furniture sizes

Every piece resizes to the centimetre, and you can draw your own pieces at any size.

Upright, in 16 languages

Scooch works in portrait, the way you hold a phone, in 16 languages. On iPhone, Keyplan 3D runs in landscape only, in 10.

Room to open it, not just to stand it

Scooch draws each wardrobe door's swing, each drawer's pull-out and a sofa bed opened out, so you can see whether the door clears the desk and the drawer clears the bed, not only whether the piece fits.

About Scooch

A 2D room planner for checking that furniture fits, before anything heavy moves.

  • Rooms drawn at their real measurements, joined into an L or a U
  • 37 everyday pieces at real sizes, each resized to the centimetre
  • A drawing tool for any other piece, at any size
  • Overlaps and pieces outside the room outlined in red
  • Wardrobe doors, drawers and sofa beds drawn with the space they need to open
  • PNG and PDF export, metric or imperial, 26 themes
  • iPhone and iPad, synced through your own iCloud

What we saw using Keyplan 3D

On 23 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • On iPhone it only runs sideways, in landscape.
  • It opened on a tutorial, then a binder of projects with four demo plans, each behind a padlock.
  • Its shop sells the Standard version once, with every project tool and as many rooms as you want, or a starter pack with ten object packs.
  • Four drags drew a room with its size and area, and its catalogue gives each piece's size in centimetres. We did not get to test furniture on top of furniture in it.

What Scooch does not do

  • Draw angled walls. Rooms are rectangles you join into an L or a U.
  • Decorate in 3D. No paint, textures or 3D view: Scooch is about whether things fit.
  • Sell packs. $9.99 once covers every piece, with nothing extra to buy.

What recent reviews of Keyplan 3D mention

From the 50 most recent US App Store reviews of Keyplan 3D, read on 23 October 2026. These are individual experiences, not tests we ran, but these themes came up more than once:

  • the Lite version asking for purchases to add more rooms, floors or paint textures
  • plans lost when moving to a new phone
  • spaces that are hard to size precisely

Pricing

Keyplan 3D Lite is free to download; its Standard version is $5.99 once and object packs cost $1.99 to $2.99 each (US). Scooch is a free download; planning needs a one-time $9.99 unlock. Neither has a subscription.

Questions

Which is cheaper?

Keyplan 3D Lite: its Standard version is $5.99 once, against Scooch's $9.99. Neither has a subscription.

Does Keyplan 3D warn when furniture will not fit?

Its listing does not describe a check like that, and we did not get to test one (version 1.9.9, 23 October 2026). Scooch outlines a piece in red when it overlaps another or sticks out of the room.

Can Scooch draw angled walls?

No. Scooch rooms are rectangles; you build an L or a U by joining rooms.
