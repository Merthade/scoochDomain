Home Design 3D alternative: Home Design 3D vs Scooch

Home Design 3D plans whole houses in 3D. Scooch is for the room you already have, and whether the furniture you want fits in it.*

I used Home Design 3D faithfully in the past, and paid for it. It is a capable house designer. Today it feels slow and dated for the job I kept opening it for: I did not need hundreds of couch designs, I needed to know whether one couch fits. So I made Scooch, simpler: rooms at their real size, furniture you resize to the centimetre or draw yourself at its real size, and a red outline when something does not fit.

Emils, who makes Scooch

Where Home Design 3D is stronger

Whole houses

Several floors, adjustable wall thickness, height and angles, roofs, and a 3D walkthrough with VR export.

Thousands of items

Far more than Scooch's 37 everyday pieces, with sizes you can change: 584 under Living room alone in our test. Handy for styling a house; for checking one sofa against one wall, it is mostly scrolling.

Blueprints

Import an existing plan and draw over it.

Where Scooch is stronger

A warning before you lift anything

Scooch outlines in red a piece that overlaps another or sticks out of the room, so a misfit shows on the plan, not on moving day. In our test, in Home Design 3D's own furnished sample house, we moved the lounge table onto the sofa and it said nothing.

One price

$9.99 once. Home Design 3D is a subscription, and recent reviews describe earlier one-time purchases moving to it.

No tracking

Home Design 3D's App Store privacy label declares data used to track you, and it asked to track us on first launch; Scooch's label declares none.

Room to open it, not just to stand it

Scooch draws each wardrobe door's swing, each drawer's pull-out and a sofa bed opened out, so you can see whether the door clears the desk and the drawer clears the bed, not only whether the piece fits.

About Scooch

A 2D room planner for checking that furniture fits, before anything heavy moves.

  • Rooms drawn at their real measurements, joined into an L or a U
  • 37 everyday pieces at real sizes, each resized to the centimetre
  • A drawing tool for any other piece, at any size
  • Overlaps and pieces outside the room outlined in red
  • Wardrobe doors, drawers and sofa beds drawn with the space they need to open
  • PNG and PDF export, metric or imperial, 26 themes
  • iPhone and iPad, synced through your own iCloud

What we saw using Home Design 3D

On 23 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • It opened with Apple's tracking request, then a page asking whether we would like to create an account. Later skips it.
  • A room in its 2D view took one drag, with the wall lengths drawn inside and out. Furniture comes from a large catalogue, 584 items under Living room alone, each with its size.
  • In its furnished sample house we moved the lounge table onto the sofa. Nothing turned red and nothing warned us. (Earlier, in an empty room, two sofas dropped on the same spot, half through a wall, went unflagged too.)
  • We could not see its free version. The iPhone we used is signed in with our own Apple ID, which bought Home Design 3D years ago, and every paid feature we tried was open. Its prices here come from the US purchase list.

What Scooch does not do

  • Plan houses or floors. Scooch plans rooms in 2D. For a whole house over several floors in 3D, Home Design 3D is built for that.
  • Offer hundreds of sofas. 37 everyday pieces you resize to the centimetre, and a drawing tool for anything else at its real size. The question is whether yours fits, not which one to pick.
  • Ask for a subscription, an account or your data. $9.99 once, 0 ads, nothing to sign up for.

What recent reviews of Home Design 3D mention

From the 100 most recent US App Store reviews of Home Design 3D, read on 23 October 2026. These are individual experiences, not tests we ran, but these themes came up more than once:

  • long-time users who bought the one-time Gold edition or content packs and lost access after the move to subscriptions
  • most items and tools locked, and ads that interrupt projects
  • crashes after recent updates, including projects that no longer open

Pricing

Home Design 3D is free to download; premium is a subscription at $4.99 a week, $12.99 to $22.99 a month or $44.99 to $79.99 a year (US). We could not check its free version ourselves: our test iPhone's Apple ID bought it years ago. Scooch is a free download; planning needs a one-time $9.99 unlock.

Questions

Does Scooch do several floors or 3D?

No. Scooch plans rooms in 2D. You can keep several plans, but not floors stacked in 3D.

Does Home Design 3D warn when furniture will not fit?

Not in our test (version 5.10, 23 October 2026): in its own furnished sample house, a table moved onto the sofa was not flagged. Scooch outlines both pieces in red.

Is either a one-time purchase?

Scooch is: $9.99 once. Home Design 3D's US purchase list shows only weekly, monthly and yearly plans.
