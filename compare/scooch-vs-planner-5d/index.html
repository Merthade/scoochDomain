Where Planner 5D is stronger

3D and renders

Planner 5D switches between 2D and 3D and renders photo-like images. Scooch is 2D only.

A huge catalogue

Over 8,400 furniture and decor items with premium, against Scooch's 37 everyday pieces. On the free tier, about half the sofas we saw were locked.

Camera capture and AI

It can capture a room in 3D with the phone's camera and generate designs with AI.

More platforms and languages

It runs across platforms and on Vision Pro, in 24 languages to Scooch's 16.

Where Scooch is stronger

A warning before you lift anything

Scooch outlines in red a piece that overlaps another or sticks out of the room. Planner 5D offered us 'Test what furniture fits in a room' as a goal during setup, then, in a living room its own wizard had furnished, let us put the coffee table on the sofa without a word.

Exact sizes without a subscription

Every Scooch piece resizes to the centimetre with the one-time unlock. Planner 5D's listing says its free version has no furniture customization.

One price, no account

$9.99 once, nothing to sign up for. Planner 5D asked for an account before the first room, then offered a free trial of a yearly plan, and when we closed that, a one-time discount that disappears once closed.

No tracking

Planner 5D's App Store privacy label declares data used to track you, and it asked to track us before the first screen; Scooch's label declares none.

Room to open it, not just to stand it

Scooch draws each wardrobe door's swing, each drawer's pull-out and a sofa bed opened out, so you can see whether the door clears the desk and the drawer clears the bed, not only whether the piece fits.

About Scooch

A 2D room planner for checking that furniture fits, before anything heavy moves.

  • Rooms drawn at their real measurements, joined into an L or a U
  • 37 everyday pieces at real sizes, each resized to the centimetre
  • A drawing tool for any other piece, at any size
  • Overlaps and pieces outside the room outlined in red
  • Wardrobe doors, drawers and sofa beds drawn with the space they need to open
  • PNG and PDF export, metric or imperial, 26 themes
  • iPhone and iPad, synced through your own iCloud

What we saw using Planner 5D

On 23 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • Before anything else it asked to send notifications and to track us, then showed its terms.
  • Next came 'Create an account to save all your projects', with no way past it. We signed in.
  • Three questions followed. One of the goals it offers is 'Test what furniture fits in a room'.
  • Then its paywall: a 7-day free trial, then a yearly plan. Closing it brought up a one-time offer of 80% off the first year, gone once closed.

  • Work in 3D or render. Scooch is 2D: it shows real sizes, door swings and the space drawers need, not a 3D view.
  • Ask for an account. Nothing to sign up for; plans sync through your own iCloud.
  • Run trials or countdown offers. $9.99 once, the same for everyone.

Questions

Is Planner 5D free?

It is free to download with a limited catalogue, but it asked us for an account before the first room. On the free tier about half the sofas we saw were locked, and its listing says furniture customization needs premium.

Does Scooch work in 3D?

No. Scooch is 2D. It shows real sizes, door swings and the space drawers and sofa beds need, but not a 3D view.

Which one tells me if a sofa will fit?

Scooch outlines furniture in red when it overlaps or sticks out of the room. In our test (Planner 5D 5.13.1, 23 October 2026), a coffee table moved onto the sofa was not flagged.
